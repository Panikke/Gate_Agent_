# The shift ladder

Each shift adds one complication and keeps all the earlier ones. Every shift, including the training day, also has live disruptions: 1 on shift 1, 2 on shifts 2 and 3, 3 on shifts 4 and 5, and up to 5 from shift 6. Decision time drops from 60 seconds to 25 as you climb.

| Shift | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| Training day | The data from the original planning exercise, unchanged |
| 1 | A generated wave, 10 check-in desks, one stand or gate opening late |
| 2 | Only 8 check-in desks |
| 3 | Three ground service teams |
| 4 | A stand or gate closed for 40 to 60 minutes in the middle of the wave |
| 5 | Late-arriving aircraft, which push departures closer together |
| 6 and above | Everything at once, with more flights and tighter gaps. From shift 7 only 7 desks; from shift 8 two closures |

## Medals

Every wave has three targets, shown in the score strip.

| Medal | How to get it |
| --- | --- |
| Bronze | Score more than the house controller |
| Silver | Score at least halfway between the house planner and gold. **Silver clears the shift and unlocks the next one** |
| Gold | Match the game's own solver, which plays the shift like you: it plans carefully, then re-plans at each disruption without knowing what comes next |

Silver is always at least 40 points above the house controller, unless gold itself is closer. The game also skips any wave where even the solver cannot beat the house by 20 points, so every shift you are given has room to do better. Gold is not always the best possible plan, so you may beat it.

## Getting stuck

Press **New wave** for a different set of flights at the same shift. Your best medal for the shift is kept.
